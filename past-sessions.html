2026

  1. Leeds, England (IMC 2026)

    UK
    Friday, 10 July 2026 · University of Leeds · Fee £140 · Ten bursaries

    The second UK-based DMSI, held during the International Medieval Congress, with five workshops, lightning talks and an evening reception.

    Workshops
    • TEI for Beginners: Encoding Text and Extracting DataSebastian Dows-Miller, University College London. Core skills for TEI-XML, the standard for digitally representing historical documents. Encode text and metadata with attention to medieval texts and manuscript descriptions, and extract metadata from existing catalogues.
    • Using Local LLMs and VLMs: Prompting, Structuring, and Automating with Medieval DataDelphine Demelas, University of Southampton. Install and run language and vision–language models locally, design prompts for historical language tasks, and draft descriptions of medieval illuminations, with ethics and critical evaluation throughout.
    • Nodegoat Curious: Building a Custom Relational Database for Your ResearchPim van Bree and Geert Kessels, LAB1100; Jesse W. Torgerson, Wesleyan University. Led by the platform's creators with a scholar who uses it in research collaborations. Learn the principles of relational databases, then build a bespoke data model for your own project.
    • Artificial Intelligence: Image Analysis Applied to Medieval ManuscriptsDominique Stutzmann, IRHT, CNRS, Paris. Using books of hours as a case study, try computer-based image analysis to detect miniatures and iconographic cycles, then apply handwritten text recognition and text analysis to find patterns and anomalies.
    • Manuscript Materiality in a Digital WorldDot Porter, Schoenberg Institute for Manuscript Studies. Examine manuscripts from Leeds University Libraries' Cultural Collections, then work with digital images, video and the VCEditor collation-modelling tool.

  2. Kalamazoo, Michigan (ICMS 2026)

    US
    Wednesday, 13 May 2026 · Western Michigan University · Fee $125 · Up to ten awards

    The first Kalamazoo-based DMSI, held during the International Congress on Medieval Studies, with five workshops, two lightning sessions and a closing reception.

    Workshops
    • Building a Virtual Medieval PilgrimageKalani Craig, University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign. A public digital history workshop on creating a web-based global medieval pilgrimage, with pre-created, historically inspired routes that can be mapped onto a local environment.
    • From Digitized Manuscript to Digital EditionLaura Morreale, Independent Scholar, and Nicholas Laiacona, Performant Software. Learn the steps from a digital manuscript image to an online edition.
    • 3D Modeling from Photo and VideoMatthew Davis, Independent Scholar. Techniques for photographing medieval material objects in interior and exterior spaces, processing images in the Meshroom visual programming framework, and creating 3D objects for online display.
    • Introduction to Handwritten Text RecognitionTobias Hodel, University of Bern. An introduction to automatic transcription and the open-source platform eScriptorium, with time to try it on your own materials.
    • Manuscript Description and Research in the Digital EcosystemL. P. Coladangelo, Digital Scriptorium, and Lynn Ransom, Schoenberg Institute for Manuscript Studies. A hands-on workshop using manuscripts from WMU's Special Collections, introducing online resources for manuscript description, cataloguing and provenance research.
