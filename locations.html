DMSI 2026, US Edition

The first Kalamazoo-based DMSI, held during the International Congress on Medieval Studies at Western Michigan University.

Date
Wednesday, 13 May 2026
Venue
Western Michigan University
Participation fee
$125
Awards
Up to ten

The day-long programme introduces a range of digital methodologies in use in medieval studies and offers in-depth exploration through one of five workshops. A group meeting opens the day, lightning talks run at midday, and a closing session offers time to network. The participation fee is in addition to the Congress registration fees.

Workshops

Building a Virtual Medieval Pilgrimage

Kalani Craig, University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign

A public digital history workshop on creating a web-based global medieval pilgrimage, with pre-created, historically inspired routes that can be mapped onto a local environment.

From Digitized Manuscript to Digital Edition

Laura Morreale, Independent Scholar, and Nicholas Laiacona, Performant Software

Learn the steps from a digital manuscript image to an online edition.

3D Modeling from Photo and Video

Matthew Davis, Independent Scholar

Techniques for photographing medieval material objects in interior and exterior spaces, processing images in the Meshroom visual programming framework, and creating 3D objects for online display.

Introduction to Handwritten Text Recognition

Tobias Hodel, University of Bern

An introduction to automatic transcription and the open-source platform eScriptorium, with time to try it on your own materials.

Manuscript Description and Research in the Digital Ecosystem

L. P. Coladangelo, Digital Scriptorium, and Lynn Ransom, Schoenberg Institute for Manuscript Studies

A hands-on workshop using manuscripts from WMU's Special Collections, introducing online resources for manuscript description, cataloguing and provenance research.

DMSI 2026, UK Edition

The second UK-based DMSI, held during the International Medieval Congress at the University of Leeds.

Date
Friday, 10 July 2026
Venue
University of Leeds
Participation fee
£140
Bursaries
Ten fee waivers

The day-long programme introduces a range of digital methodologies in use in medieval studies and offers in-depth exploration through one of five workshops. A group meeting opens the day, lightning talks run at midday, and a wrap-up session offers time to network. The fee is in addition to IMC registration, but attending the Congress is not necessary to take part in DMSI.

Workshops

TEI for Beginners: Encoding Text and Extracting Data

Sebastian Dows-Miller, University College London

Core skills for TEI-XML, the standard for digitally representing historical documents. Encode text and metadata with attention to medieval texts and manuscript descriptions, and extract metadata from existing catalogues.

Using Local LLMs and VLMs: Prompting, Structuring, and Automating with Medieval Data

Delphine Demelas, University of Southampton

Install and run language and vision–language models locally, design prompts for historical language tasks, and draft descriptions of medieval illuminations, with ethics and critical evaluation throughout.

Nodegoat Curious: Building a Custom Relational Database for Your Research

Pim van Bree and Geert Kessels, LAB1100; Jesse W. Torgerson, Wesleyan University

Led by the platform's creators with a scholar who uses it in research collaborations. Learn the principles of relational databases, then build a bespoke data model for your own project.

Artificial Intelligence: Image Analysis Applied to Medieval Manuscripts

Dominique Stutzmann, IRHT, CNRS, Paris

Using books of hours as a case study, try computer-based image analysis to detect miniatures and iconographic cycles, then apply handwritten text recognition and text analysis to find patterns and anomalies.

Manuscript Materiality in a Digital World

Dot Porter, Schoenberg Institute for Manuscript Studies

Examine manuscripts from Leeds University Libraries' Cultural Collections, then work with digital images, video and the VCEditor collation-modelling tool.
